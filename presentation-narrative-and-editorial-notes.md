# Lots in Commons

## Seven-minute presentation script and editorial notes

**Status:** Working narrative, third pass (redraft)

**Deck:** 9 vertically navigated slides

**Target:** 6:30–6:50 at approximately 145 words per minute

**Long-form archive:** `presentation-narrative-and-editorial-notes-long-form.md`

## The argument in one sentence

Forecasts do not merely describe uncertain futures: institutions use them to reorganize land, capacity, value, and perception in the present; this project asks whether situated sensing and representational friction can keep those translations open to contest.

## What changed in this pass

- **Named the 5→6 bridge.** Both the mushroom-ecology material and the AI-training material are now framed explicitly as the same question—what counts as ground truth when a system recurses on itself—so the jump reads as a change of scale, not a change of subject.
- **Cut Slide 5.** It carried too much: the forecast/ecology contrast, "modern alchemy," Haraway, Tsing, Khan, and *Rare Earthenware* in one breath. Haraway and Tsing remain as the counter-epistemology; "modern alchemy" now gets room to breathe instead of arriving as an assertion.
- **Moved the remainder idea to Slide 3.** *Rare Earthenware*'s core move—transformation carries its toxicity forward—now grounds Slide 3's "remainder" line materially, rather than repeating itself two slides later.
- **Reincorporated Nora Khan / *HOLO 3*—at Slide 6, not Slide 5.** Her ecological-adjacent framing in the old Slide 5 was redundant with Haraway/Tsing, so that placement is still cut. But her partial-knowing/explainability frame is the right theoretical anchor for Slide 6's closing move: it argues that withholding legibility from a predictive system is a form of agency, not just an absence. Without Khan, "the right to remain unread" was asserted; with her, it's argued. Glissant, Stengers, Hui, and Campagna remain in the Field for later development.
- **Slide 4 drafted two ways.** Version A stays closer to the current wearable-apparatus framing; Version B leans harder into groundwater as the primary sensing register, with the body as instrument rather than subject. Pick one before the next visual pass.
- **Slide 8 rewritten** to point at the physical-object / projection / drawing composition, not a generic list of media, and to make *Computational Compost* the clear precedent for that method.
- **Overall trim** to roughly 950–980 spoken words, tightening sentences a listener has to decode in real time while preserving register and cadence.

## Three threads—and the question that binds them

1. **Prediction becomes infrastructure.** Predictive markets organize expectations; Keller Easterling helps ask how those expectations acquire spatial agency through standards, incentives, zones, and repeatable formulas.
2. **Other ways of knowing remain active.** Donna Haraway and Anna Tsing help resist the idea that partiality is simply a defect to be engineered away.
3. **Images do work.** Hito Steyerl and Jussi Parikka help shift the image from representation alone toward circulation, classification, measurement, and action.

The shared question is not whether computation is good or bad. It is: **which ways of knowing gain the authority to organize matter, which are treated as remainder, and how might representation redistribute that authority?**

## Slide 1 — Lots in Commons

**Time:** 0:00–0:30

**Visual role:** Establish the title as an open territory. No explanatory precedent is needed.

### Script

*Lots in Commons* is a working title for a research territory. "Lots" names parcels and abundance, but also division. "Commons" names the systems collective life relies upon—even when their governance is anything but common.

The project begins with an unease: uncertain futures become concrete long before they become certain. An expectation becomes a line on a map; soon the line becomes a contract, a substation, a landscape. I want to follow that hardening and ask how it might remain negotiable.

## Slide 2 — Forecasts are infrastructure before they are buildings

**Time:** 0:30–1:15

**Visual role:** Paglen grounds an apparently planetary network; the ERCOT map shows capacity as an actionable field.

### Script

Infrastructure is usually treated as background. I approach it as the medium where matter, law, finance, and expectation meet. A forecast does not simply report a possible future—it changes what appears investable, what receives permission, what is prepared to connect.

Keller Easterling's *Extrastatecraft* gives me a language for this spatial agency: markets price expected demand, but infrastructure space carries that expectation outward, settling into standards and repeatable formulas until a speculative future looks like an ordinary course of action.

Paglen's cable landing sites and the ERCOT map show the moment abstraction touches ground. Prediction becomes infrastructural when institutions can act through it before the event it anticipates has arrived.

### Editorial note

Do not claim Easterling writes directly about prediction markets—her role is the infrastructure space through which priced expectations become permissions and material commitments. One network image and one grounded site are enough.

## Slide 3 — Prediction becomes material through translation

**Time:** 1:15–2:10

**Visual role:** The network is the project's analytical diagram. Each translation separates category, process, and actor/instrument into linked nodes; the four branches converge on one shared site while the outer connections make circular causality visible.

### Script

The primary question is: how do institutions translate uncertain futures into sites and material commitments?

I am following four interacting translations. A projection becomes an obligation. Ground becomes value. Terrain becomes a score. A resource becomes a promise. These are not neutral stages; they reinforce one another. Demand justifies capacity. Promised capacity alters land value. Value directs selection, and the selected site produces a new story of demand that appears to confirm where the cycle began.

At every turn, authority is redistributed. Who produces the forecast, who acts on it, who absorbs its consequences? Beneath the network lies a remainder—everything that persists after it stops counting as evidence. A mineral becomes a component the way ground becomes value; the toxicity that made the component possible does not disappear because the ledger stops counting it.

### Editorial note

Let the network carry the causal structure. Pause after the main question, then follow one branch from category to process to actor/instrument before moving quickly through the other three. The closing "remainder" line now does double duty—read it slowly, since it will recur materially in Slide 6 and 7.

## Slide 4 — North Texas is an aperture, not a model

**Time:** 2:10–2:55

**Visual role:** Move from institutional vision to embodied and material sensing.

### Script — Version A (wearable apparatus foregrounded)

North Texas remains useful as an aperture rather than a universal case. At its suburban–rural edge, competing futures for the same ground become unusually visible—a field can be read as agriculture, subdivision, logistics territory, or computational capacity.

The question becomes one of sensing. Administrative maps register parcels with precision while flattening what moves beneath and across them. The wearable apparatus brings the sensing body into the frame as an instrument, not a witness standing outside the site. Anthony Acciavatti's *Grounded Growth* treats groundwater as urban form rather than hidden resource—together they ask what becomes perceptible when measurement is situated, and whose exposure counts as evidence.

### Script — Version B (groundwater foregrounded)

North Texas remains useful as an aperture rather than a universal case. At its suburban–rural edge, the same ground supports agriculture, subdivision, logistics territory, and computational capacity depending only on who is asking.

Anthony Acciavatti's *Grounded Growth* gives me the sharper instrument: groundwater as urban form, a substrate that administrative maps register only as a parcel line. What moves beneath the surface does not respect that line. The wearable apparatus follows the same logic downward into the body—sensing is situated, partial, and carried, not surveyed from above. The question is what becomes perceptible only at that scale, and whose exposure counts as evidence.

### Editorial note

Confirm the author and title of the wearable apparatus before a formal review; keep its caption descriptive rather than authoritative until then. Choose A or B based on which image is stronger in hand—A keeps the two precedents as parallel instruments, B makes groundwater the spine and folds the body into it.

## Slide 5 — A forecast is only one way of knowing a future

**Time:** 2:55–3:40

**Visual role:** A distributed collage connects ecological counter-reading and the limits of computation, without treating them as equal categories.

### Script

The contrast is not rational computation versus irrational ecology. It is a difference in attention. A forecast isolates variables, prices risk, searches for what can be repeated. Haraway asks us to stay with damaged entanglements instead of resolving them. Tsing follows relations that resist scalability and nevertheless sustain life.

Call this a modern alchemy: not mysticism, but a willingness to work obliquely with transformation, holding a process without demanding that it yield a clean, repeatable output. A forecast wants the mushroom to become a model. Tsing's mushroom refuses—and stays a mushroom, entangled, unrepeatable, alive.

The same refusal will resurface at a different scale in a moment: what happens when a system can no longer tell its own residue from the ground it started with.

### Editorial note

This is the seam repair. The last line is the bridge—say it deliberately, then let the transition to Slide 6 land as a scale shift rather than a topic change. *Rare Earthenware* has moved to Slide 3; do not reintroduce it here. Keep Glissant, Stengers, Hui, Campagna, and Khan in the Field for later development.

## Slide 6 — When models train on models, human data becomes scarce ground truth

**Time:** 3:40–4:30

**Visual role:** The *Anatomy of AI* diagram shows material systems; *ImageNet Roulette* shows recognition as classification and harm.

### Script

The same question—what a system can no longer distinguish from its own residue—returns in AI training. Human expression becomes raw material; generated output circulates back into the corpus. Institutions then decide which human traces still qualify as provenance, as ground truth.

Research on model collapse shows that indiscriminate recursion can narrow a distribution until its edges disappear. Collapse is not inevitable when original data is retained and mixtures are curated—but that makes the politics sharper: who controls the mixture, who defines the real? Nora Khan's *HOLO 3* names partial knowing and explainability as limits, not defects prediction should eventually correct.

*ImageNet Roulette* reminds us that legibility is not automatically liberating. Recognition can misname and injure. Agency may mean correction; sometimes, following Khan, it means the right to remain unread.

### Editorial note

Use "can," not "will," when discussing collapse—this keeps the claim technically defensible and keeps the slide on curation and authority, not doom. *HOLO 3*'s cover now sits as a third card alongside *Anatomy of an AI System* and *ImageNet Roulette*—keep it visually secondary (smaller, between the other two) since it is conceptual ballast for the closing question, not a third co-equal precedent.

## Slide 7 — What an image fails to resolve may still be evidence

**Time:** 4:30–5:20

**Visual role:** A three-part collage links glitch, atmospheric evidence, and machine-operational vision.

### Script

Representation is not downstream from these systems—images participate in decisions about the world. Jussi Parikka extends the operational image toward pictures made by and for systems to act, often without a human viewer. Sensing stretches beyond the visible, making certain relations computable while others fall away.

Hito Steyerl's poor image introduces a different politics: low resolution is not only loss, it records the pressure of circulation and value. Omar Mismar lets corruption break across the image's surface. Forensic Architecture's *Cloud Studies* works in the other direction, gathering atmospheric traces until violence becomes perceptible.

Glitch should not be romanticized, but it can reveal the seam where signal becomes decision. Can remote sensing be reclaimed as situated witnessing rather than extraction?

### Editorial note

This is the poetic hinge of the presentation. Let the images remain visible after the final question; do not narrate every tile.

## Slide 8 — Composition becomes a method of investigation

**Time:** 5:20–6:05

**Visual role:** A physical object, a projection, and a drawing in conversation—not a media inventory.

### Script

The project takes form less as an atlas than as a composition. A drawing traces what an image cannot hold. A physical object gives a translation weight and duration—something you could, in principle, still hold after the forecast that produced it has been forgotten. A projection lets that object be re-read against the site it came from, so the two accounts can be caught disagreeing with each other in real time.

Marina Otero Verzier's *Computational Compost* is the precedent: it does not represent infrastructure meeting metabolism, it stages their actual contact within one experiment, and the argument emerges from that contact rather than from a caption beside it.

I want to work the same way—object, projection, and drawing as three instruments in the same room, not illustrations of a thesis decided in advance. The composition is where evidence gets staged, propositions get tested, and a new question is allowed to appear.

### Editorial note

Resist turning this into an inventory of media. Replace "a physical object," "a projection," "a drawing" with the actual pieces—cast fragment, projected site footage, working drawing—as soon as they exist. The point is contact between specific things, not a category list.

## Slide 9 — Open proposition

**Time:** 6:05–6:30

**Visual role:** End on one question and white space.

### Script

The project does not propose that every forecast should stop, or that every system can remain reversible. It asks what happens at the moment uncertainty hardens into authority—and whether representation can reopen that moment.

The working proposition is therefore: **how might representation keep a future contestable before prediction becomes commitment?**

### Editorial note

Stop after the question. The Field and Index links can remain visible for discussion but should not be verbally introduced.
