## Core project proposition

At the metropolitan edge of North Texas, forecasts do not merely describe growth. They reorganize land, water, electricity, ownership, finance, and perception before construction begins.

The project is not simply “Texas landscape versus technology.” Its subject is the conversion of uncertain futures into present-day spatial commitments.

## Central research gap

Real-estate, electricity, water, transportation, population, and ecological forecasts are usually studied in separate institutional records even when they converge on the same parcel.

Working question: **How do forecasts made in incompatible systems converge on the same landscape, and what material changes occur before any forecast is proven true?**

## Strong narrative device

Treat the parcel as a prediction and follow it through five simultaneous states:

1. **Ground** — prairie, pasture, drainage, heat, animals, soil, labor, memory.
2. **Survey** — coordinates, title history, fence, appraisal, lot line, aerial image.
3. **Forecast** — population, traffic, electrical load, water demand, future value.
4. **Commitment** — option agreement, plat, utility extension, road, transformer, debt.
5. **Atmosphere** — server heat, stormwater, advertising image, cloud computation, speculative residue.

## Four translations that make a site actionable

- **Property:** boundary → title → value
- **Suitability:** terrain → score → selection
- **Capacity:** resource → service → commitment
- **Demand:** projection → obligation → build

These are not four historical lineages. They are four operating translations through which a landscape becomes available to development.

## Presentation arc

**Proposition → forecasting mechanism → research question → four translations → North Texas → counter-reading → methods → precedents → representation → provisional claim → capstone → negotiation**

The argument should accumulate causally rather than proceed as a list of research categories.

## Counter-reading

Computation and divination can be treated as competing systems for making futures actionable. The political distinction is not that one is simply true and the other false, but that only one currently has the authority to finance roads, reserve electricity, divide land, and define future value.

Working questions:

- Why is one technique of prediction empowered to transform land while another is dismissed as superstition?
- What does the computational forecast fail to perceive, and what does it inherit from divination?

## Poster and projection

- Build a deep oblique sectional axonometric in Rhino.
- Establish and lock the final view early.
- Use Make2D in separate registered passes for ground, parcels, utilities, infrastructure, forecast, and subsurface conditions.
- Use Illustrator to distinguish evidence, inference, approved development, forecasts, ecological processes, and speculative futures.
- Let the poster hold multiple possible states simultaneously.
- Let the projection continually select, suppress, and recombine those states.

The poster becomes an archive of possible futures; the projection behaves like the mechanism assigning them changing probabilities.

## Editorial cautions

- Keep North Texas specific; avoid generic Texas or ranching imagery.
- Use one parcel or a five-to-ten-mile transect rather than the entire metroplex.
- Do not introduce the international border unless a specific supply chain requires it.
- Treat ranching through concrete practices: leases, agricultural valuation, fences, ponds, grazing cycles, and interim landholding.
- Quotes should remain sparse, linked, and subordinate to the project’s argument.
- Precedents should demonstrate methods rather than decorate topical slides.
- Occult or alchemical imagery must perform an analytical operation; otherwise it risks overwhelming the site evidence.
