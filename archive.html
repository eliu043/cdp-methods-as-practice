01 Research territoryWhat questions can sustain several possible projects without becoming boundless?

Contemporary infrastructures are frequently narrated as inevitable responses to demand: more data requires more computation; more computation requires more energy, land, water, minerals, logistics, and labor.

This inquiry asks how those chains of necessity are assembled—and how their material consequences are distributed across sites that may appear unrelated.

How do computational, financial, logistical, and ecological projections make planetary infrastructures appear necessary, governable, and inevitable?

Supporting questions

  • How does an uncertain forecast become an actionable claim on matter, territory, and collective resources?
  • What appears in the parallax between institutional, remote, financial, ecological, and lived representations?
  • What does a computational system exclude, and who encounters the consequences of that absence?
  • Can an atlas support negotiation rather than optimization?

Research tendrils

These are not separate topics yet. They are paths through a shared question: how computational systems become material, spatial, and political.

  • Grounding the cloudFollow computation through energy, water, minerals, land, logistics, labor, heat, and waste.
  • Forecast territoriesStudy how projections of demand act upon the world before the futures they describe have arrived.
  • Parallax between systemsCompare remote, administrative, financial, ecological, and embodied versions of the same infrastructure.
  • Protocols of inevitabilityExamine standards, incentives, classifications, interfaces, and models that make expansion appear natural.
  • Maintenance and refusalAsk what becomes visible when breakdown, care, latency, opacity, and the right not to calculate become central.
  • The performable atlasTurn mapping from a distant description into an instrument that visitors inhabit, alter, and negotiate.

04Historical lineagesWhat longer trajectories make contemporary infrastructure legible?

Land made calculable

Cadastral surveys, property boundaries, valuation, zoning, and suitability analysis turned situated landscapes into comparable and governable units.

The operational image

Aerial photography, satellites, sensors, GIS, computer vision, and digital twins increasingly produce images intended to trigger decisions rather than simply represent.

Networks that claim to disappear

Telegraph, electricity, fiber optics, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence depend on situated systems while cultivating narratives of frictionless connection.

Architecture as financial instrument

Forecasting, standardization, risk models, and financialization reorganize buildings and landscapes around anticipated value.

05Community of practiceWhose work provides concepts, methods, and productive disagreements?

Mél Hogan makes data infrastructure materially and geographically specific through water, energy, land, surveillance, and local economies.

Kate Crawford positions artificial intelligence within planetary systems of extraction, labor, classification, and power.

Keller Easterling studies the standards, zones, incentives, and spatial formulas through which infrastructure exercises power.

Laura Kurgan treats spatial data and mapping technologies as situated political arguments rather than neutral views.

Forensic Architecture assembles models, imagery, testimony, sensing, and fieldwork into navigable spatial evidence.

Center for Spatial Research provides a model for joining data rigor with critical reflection on the ideologies and limits of representation.

The project sits between critical cartography, material investigation, computational experimentation, and public encounter.

06Situated planetary systemsHow can a planetary inquiry remain accountable to particular grounds?

Planetary does not mean viewing the world from nowhere, nor does it require mapping everything. It names relationships that exceed a single site while remaining material in every location they touch.

A cable landing, mine, warehouse, server hall, substation, cooling system, logistics corridor, waste site, and user interface may belong to one infrastructure while producing radically different local conditions.

Each investigation should move between scales while remaining answerable to selected sites, bodies, ecologies, and forms of testimony.

07MethodsWhich operations can make distributed systems perceptible without pretending to total knowledge?
  • Critical mapping across network, regional, and situated scales
  • Satellite imagery, land-cover histories, LiDAR, and remote-sensing products
  • Energy grids, interconnection queues, substations, and demand forecasts
  • Fiber routes, cable landings, logistics networks, and data-center inventories
  • Water systems, watersheds, cooling technologies, and withdrawal records
  • Mineral supply chains, construction materials, waste, and embodied energy
  • Corporate reports, regulatory filings, standards, permits, and incentives
  • Field observation, interviews, testimony, maintenance records, and situated sensing
  • Comparative models that preserve disagreement, uncertainty, and unknowns
08Computational and material experimentsHow can material alter what computation knows—and computation alter what is made?

A useful feedback loop:

material apparatus → computational perception → error or uncertainty → drawing or fabrication → revised apparatus

Possible experiments include layered parallax viewers, misregistered maps, optical filters, projection, spatialized data, slow interfaces, physical network models, performative simulations, and evidentiary drawings.

09Visual languageWhat aesthetic modes can operate as sensory investigation rather than decoration?

The visual language may combine the apparent precision of technical documents with interruption, misregistration, translucency, opacity, accumulation, and blank space.

Maps should reveal their instruments, dates, resolutions, sources, omissions, and positions. Beauty can invite attention, but uncertainty should not be aestheticized into an unexplained glitch.

10Rhetorical argumentWhat does this territory currently argue?

Planetary infrastructure is often represented as the natural consequence of technological demand. This project argues that expansion becomes actionable only after institutions translate uncertain futures into forecasts, classifications, protocols, financial commitments, and suitable grounds.

These futures are neither frictionless nor inevitable. They are materialized through particular landscapes, resources, labor, and forms of governance.

By placing incompatible representations beside one another, the project asks whether computation can make conflict and partial knowledge discussable rather than manufacturing consensus.

11Possible thesis formsSeveral forms may emerge from the same research territory.
  • An interactive and performable atlas of a distributed infrastructure
  • A parallax instrument comparing incompatible regimes of sensing and classification
  • A covenant interface that delays, conditions, or refuses calculation
  • An evidentiary reconstruction moving between network and situated scales
  • A material installation that exposes the grounds of an apparently immaterial system
  • A slow observatory organized around maintenance, latency, breakdown, and care
12Productive risksWhat must remain unresolved at this stage?
  • Scale: planetary scope can become vague unless each inquiry lands in particular evidence and ground.
  • Totalization: the atlas must disclose partiality rather than simulate an all-seeing system.
  • Aestheticization: beautiful infrastructure images can conceal the politics they are meant to expose.
  • Technical seduction: tools should open cultural and political questions rather than close them with performance metrics.
  • Premature thesis: the present goal is a fertile territory with testable tendrils, not an answer chosen too early.
13Counter-computationWhat other practices read futures, relations, and transformations without reducing them to forecasts?

Mysticism, animism, divination, and alchemy should not become an aesthetic escape from computation. They are more useful here as counter-methods: practices that challenge universal legibility, separation, instrumentality, and the belief that reality is exhausted by what can be measured.

Incomputable knowledge

Nora N. Khan examines the epistemic limits of machine vision and algorithmic knowledge, asking how technical systems reorganize perception and naming.

Technic and magic

Federico Campagna opposes Technic’s absolute language with an ineffable remainder that cannot become a unit or instrument.

Reclaiming animism

Isabelle Stengers treats animism and magic as situated arts of relation and assemblage rather than supernatural explanations.

Opacity and cosmotechnics

Édouard Glissant provides an ethics of opacity, while Yuk Hui asks how plural cosmologies might produce different technical practices.

Poethical reading

Denise Ferreira da Silva and Valentina Desideri use tarot, blacklight, sensing, and nonseparability as compositional practices beyond modern determinacy.

Historical grounding

Carolyn Merchant and Silvia Federici connect the mechanization of nature and suppression of magical or reproductive knowledges to capitalism, enclosure, gender, and control.

What does the computational forecast fail to perceive, and what does it inherit from divination?
